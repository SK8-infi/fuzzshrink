\abstract{Dastkhan, Mosaffa and Fabozzi (2026) propose a portfolio framework that combines fuzzy clustering, an LSTM forecaster and a genetic algorithm, and report that \$1,000 grew to \$5,344 in 2024 with a Sharpe ratio of 11.93 against 0.31 for the Nasdaq-100. We re-examine these claims with the same stock universe, cost assumptions and a reconstruction of the pipeline, under protocols fixed before each computation. The published performance table is internally inconsistent: reported returns exceed those implied by the reported final wealth, and the benchmark rows mix monthly and annual units; on the paper's own formula the Nasdaq-100's 2024 Sharpe ratio is 2.30. The reported 1.67\% price forecast error is worse than a random walk (1.38\%), and the LSTM's return forecasts carry no cross-sectional information. Choosing the model configuration on the test year, as the paper did, roughly doubles the 2024 result relative to choosing it on the preceding year. Over 2019--2024 with honest annual re-selection the pipeline earns high returns, but by holding about three stocks at 2.3 times the index's volatility; its Sharpe ratio (0.97) matches the index (0.93) and trails an equal-weighted portfolio of the same stocks (1.05). The paper's module-contribution claims point in the stated direction but are not statistically significant. The framework's apparent outperformance is largely a matter of risk and reporting rather than predictive skill.}
