\renewcommand\articleabstract{Dastkhan, Mosaffa and Fabozzi (2026) combine fuzzy clustering, an LSTM forecaster and a genetic algorithm into a portfolio framework and report that \$1,000 grew to \$5,344 in 2024, with a Sharpe ratio of 11.93 against 0.31 for the Nasdaq-100. We test these claims on the same stocks and cost assumptions, using a reconstruction of the pipeline and protocols fixed before each computation. The published performance table does not agree with itself. Its reported returns exceed those implied by its own final-wealth figures, and its benchmark rows mix monthly and annual units. By the paper's own formula, the Nasdaq-100's 2024 Sharpe ratio is 2.30. The reported 1.67\% price error is higher than that of a random walk (1.38\%), and the LSTM's return forecasts carry no information about which stocks will outperform. When the cluster count and selection rule are picked on 2023 rather than on 2024 itself, our reconstruction's 2024 final wealth falls from \$2,543 to \$1,201, about what QQQ earned. Re-choosing the configuration every January over 2019--2024 turns \$1,000 into roughly \$9,600, but the portfolio holds 3.2 stocks on average at 2.3 times the index's volatility, and its Sharpe ratio (0.97) is close to QQQ's (0.93) and below that of an equal-weighted portfolio of the 85 stocks (1.05). Clustering, the LSTM and the genetic algorithm each add return in most seeds, but no difference passes a HAC $t$-test at the 5\% level. Most of the reported edge comes from risk and from how results were computed and selected, with little left that forecasting skill would need to explain.}
